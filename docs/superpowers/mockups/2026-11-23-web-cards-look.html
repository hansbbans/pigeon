Pigeon web: the Cards look (part 4)

The same cards as iOS Cards (white tiles on the grouped grey, a 3px bar in the folder's colour, serif headlines, "N more"), and the same structure as the Reeder sections the web already has. The open question is the layout: where the cards sit in the browser window. Each option shows a folder (Newsletters), and A and B also show Home. The Cards | Reeder switch sits in the toolbar in all three.

A. Pages · like iPad Cards: Home, then the folder, then the story, each filling the window

No sidebar in Cards. Home is the tile grid, a tile opens its folder as a page of cards, and a story opens across the window with "‹ Newsletters" to go back. The library (every feed) is the All Feeds tiles, one click from Home. This matches iOS exactly and is the most editorial, but you lose the always-visible library and j/k move within one page at a time.

B. Library + page · the web's quiet sidebar stays; the rest of the window is one page of cards recommended

The library column you use now stays on the left, collapsible as today, with Home at its top. Everything to its right is the Cards page: Home tiles, a folder's cards, or the Front Page for a smart view. The Aggregator's dilemma, revisited for an age of agents

Ben Thompson · 6 min ago · 12 min read

Aggregation Theory was always a theory about where value accrues when distribution costs fall to zero. Agents change the question: when the user no longer visits the page at all, who is the aggregator?

The answer, at least for now, is whoever owns the interface the agent reports back to, which is why every platform is racing to be the place the agent lives.
